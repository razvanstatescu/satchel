# Satchel hypermotion video: research and three treatments

Story lead notes, 2026-10-22. Target: an X upload of **85–110 s** (hard cap 2:20 for a non-Premium account; the plan
caps it at 2:15, D-17). Mostly motion graphics with recreated or captured product UI (fixture mode today).

The product truth comes from `README.md`, `brand/`, `kb/80` (Demo video beats, HandoffAnimation, CleanHistory,
ProofChip/GateLine/FreezeBanner, Copy deck), `kb/70` TL;DR, `kb/90` §4 and §7, and `plan/decisions.md` D-32 (the
demo cast). Every claim in the scripts below maps to one of them (see "Fact check" at the end).

### Hackathon judging signals
- Colosseum: the pitch video is what judges watch first, and "a clear and well-structured narrative is more valuable
  than professional video editing". Their listed mistakes include "overly flashy visuals with little substance" and
  vague or overly technical descriptions ([Colosseum blog](https://blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/)).
  For a hypermotion cut, the motion has to *carry information*, not decorate it.
- ETHGlobal: skip dead waiting such as wallet confirmations, but do not speed the video up to fit the limit
  ([ETHGlobal event rules](https://ethglobal.com/events/ethonline2026/info/details)). That matches kb/80: jump-cut the
  signer wait **with the timer visible before and after the cut**.

### The first 5 seconds on X (muted autoplay)
- X videos autoplay **without sound** on a mostly mobile timeline. Publishers reported that 85 % of Facebook video views
  were silent ([Nieman Lab on Digiday, 2016](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/publishers-say-85-percent-of-facebook-video-is-watched-without-sound/)).
- Twitter's neuroscience study with Omnicom: videos with an **early story arc were 58 % more likely to be watched past
  3 s**; in the first 3 s, attention and memory encoding were the same with sound on or off; **front-loading the brand**
  helped recall ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/twitter-omnicom-media-group-brand-video-survey/),
  [Adweek on branding](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/front-loading-branding-makes-twitter-video-ads-more-effective-study/)).
- Google's ABCD (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction), validated on 17,000+ campaigns
  ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en)): grab attention fast and show the brand early.
- **Rules we take from this:**
  1. Frame 0 already moves, and the first card of text is legible by 0:01.
  2. The on-screen text alone tells the whole story (the VO adds rhythm and nuance, never new facts).
  3. The Satchel card (brass band = the brand) is on screen by 0:02. The wordmark follows within 5 s.
  4. There is a person or a human gesture (a hand, a handshake) early in the problem beat.
  5. Text cards are 2–5 words in sentence case (brand rule), Bricolage Grotesque, each on screen for at least
     0.8 s + 0.25 s per word.

*Method note: several pages (Medium, Zuora, x.com, socialmediatoday) could not be opened from this machine, so their
facts above come from search-result summaries. The Colosseum and Family pages were read directly.*

## 2. Shared ground rules for every treatment

**Story spine (but / therefore).** An NFT can control a wallet, **therefore** you can sell a wallet as an NFT. **But** a
signature issued before the sale can still be broadcast after it. **Therefore** Satchel writes every signature down
before it exists, pauses signing while listed, and lets the buyer's browser check every record. **But** if one could
still move coins, Buy stays disabled. **Therefore** the buyer gets the wallet with a history they checked themselves.

**Cast (D-32):** Satchel #1 (the flagship judge view), #3 (the sale: listed for 5 USDC, carol buys, EVM-only history),
#4 (deliberately "Blocked: 1 unused signature"), and the personas alice (seller), bob and carol (buyer). Dana the OTC
trader can be the implied seller in narrative beats; never invent a balance or price that the demo data won't have.

**Real product copy to put on screen (kb/80 Copy deck):** "This NFT is a wallet." · "Signing paused while listed" ·
"Checked in your browser" · "Clean. No past signature can move these coins." · "Clean history · checked 8 s ago" ·
"Blocked: 1 unused signature" · "Runs in your browser. Satchel's server isn't trusted for this." · "Satchel #3 is yours."
· "Signed by NEAR's signer network" · "Usually about 10 seconds."

**Brand in motion.**
- Night ground `#17100c` and leather surfaces (`#221812`, `#33251c`). **Brass `#d4ae55` is spent once per frame**:
  the card band, the Buy button or the stitch, never all three at full weight.
- **The stitch means "you own this"** (1.5 px, dash 6/5, inset 8). In the film it appears *only* when ownership is
  established or moves. That gives us a free, on-brand device for every treatment.
- The type is Bricolage Grotesque (kinetic cards), Instrument Sans (small supers) and Spline Sans Mono (addresses and
  hashes only), always in sentence case.
- The logo is never rotated, slanted, glowed or placed on brass. The S may not spin as a transition.
- Motion: the product's handoff (lift, 6° tilt, ≈ 700 ms spring slide, stitch draws in over 300 ms) is the hero move.
  Hypermotion energy lives in the cuts and the type, not in wobbling the logo.

**Never on screen or in VO:** "trustless" or "no trust needed"; "safe to buy in N blocks" or any countdown after
listing; "nothing was sent and no funds moved" after a timeout; mainnet; fiat (UX-1 is native only; if a number ever
appears, it must say "at mainnet prices"); "the contract checks the history" (the buyer's browser does, the app keeps
Buy disabled until it passes, and the buy is bound to the record count); "MPC" in VO (say "NEAR's signer network" or
"NEAR Chain Signatures"); a Solana *send* (the app's SOL send flow is `[TBD]`, while the SOL address is fine).

## 3. Treatment A: "Signed before the sale"

**Logline:** A wallet sale where the seller has already slipped a signed cheque into his pocket, and the NFT that makes
him show it.

**Approach:** villain, then problem-agitate-solve. **Tone:** noir-tinged thriller that turns into calm competence:
dark, close, tactile, then crisp product UI. **Target length:** 100 s.

**Central metaphor:** the **post-dated cheque**. A signature issued before the sale is a cheque signed today and
cashed tomorrow. Clean history is a ledger where every cheque is entered *before the pen touches paper*, and the buyer
checks each one is void (cashed, or expired) before shaking hands.

| Time | Beat | Picture (what we see) | VO | Kinetic text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Hook | Extreme close-up on leather-gloved hand tearing a cheque from a book, signing it, sliding it into an inside pocket. Hard cut to a handshake over a Satchel card. | "Every wallet sale has a moment like this." | "Signed before the sale." |
| 0:04–0:09 | Hook payoff | Freeze on the handshake. The cheque in the pocket glows faintly through the jacket (x-ray). The new owner walks off with the card; the cheque is cashed behind his back, coins drain from the card's chips. | "A signature the seller got before the sale. Used after it." | "Cashed after it." |
| 0:09–0:15 | Idea | Snap to Night ground. The brand card slams in, brass band "Satchel #1", chips BTC · ETH · SOL. | "Satchel is an NFT that is a wallet. And it makes the seller show every cheque." | "An NFT that is a wallet." |
| 0:15–0:24 | Problem (old way) | A seed phrase on a card gets photocopied: 12 words fan out into a stack of identical copies. | "Selling a wallet used to mean handing over a seed phrase. Anyone who saw it could keep a copy." | "Seed phrases get copied." |
| 0:24–0:32 | First fix | The phrase burns away; the card's three addresses (mono, middle-truncated) lock into the card. Only the owner's hand can lift the pen. | "An NFT fixes the copy. The contract signs only for whoever owns it." | "Only the owner signs." |
| 0:32–0:40 | The hole | Rewind: the cheque reappears in the seller's pocket, untouched by the fix. | "But a cheque signed yesterday still clears tomorrow." | "One hole left." |
| 0:40–0:45 | Turn | The buyer's hand hovers over Buy. The cursor stops. | "So the buyer has to see every one." | "Show me every cheque." |
| 0:45–1:00 | Solution 1–2 | A brass-ruled ledger. A pen moves to sign; *before it touches*, a line writes itself in the ledger (chain, nonce, coin). Then a padlocked clasp closes over the chequebook: FreezeBanner "Signing paused while listed". | "Satchel writes each signature into its record before NEAR's signer network makes it. List the Satchel and signing pauses. The ledger stops growing." | "Written before it's signed." → "Signing paused while listed" |
| 1:00–1:12 | Solution 3 | Each ledger line flies to its chain and comes back void: "Settled · nonce 3 used", "Spent in …", "Expired". The verdict sentence types on. | "Then the buyer's browser checks every line against each chain. Used. Spent. Expired." | "Checked in your browser" → "Clean. No past signature can move these coins." |
| 1:12–1:18 | Proof (negative) | Satchel #4's page: one line stays live, red triangle. GateLine "Blocked: 1 unused signature". Buy dims. | "If one cheque could still clear, Buy stays disabled." | "Blocked: 1 unused signature" |
| 1:18–1:30 | Proof (positive) | Satchel #3 on carol's screen: "Clean history · checked 8 s ago", Buy → handoff: the card lifts, tilts 6°, slides to carol; the stitch draws in. | "When every one is void, one transaction pays the seller and hands over the wallet." | "Satchel #3 is yours." |
| 1:30–1:40 | Close | The cheque in the seller's pocket dissolves into ledger dust. End card. | "Satchel. No cheques in the pocket." | End card |

- **Signature moment:** the pen hovering over the cheque while the ledger line writes itself *first*. It makes "record
  before signature" visible in under a second.
- **Music arc:** a low pulse and pizzicato in the hook (tension) → silence on "One hole left." → a drop at "Satchel
  writes each signature…" → a driving ticking groove through the checks → resolve on a single warm chord at the
  handoff.
- **Brand:** noir shots graded to leather browns (no cold blues). The ledger is ruled in brass. The stitch appears only
  at the handoff, on carol's card.
- **VO:** 149 words, about 56–60 s at 150–160 wpm. **Total runtime:** ≈ 100 s (music breaks and the end card fill the rest).
- **Risks:**
  - **Live-action cost**: the hands, cheque and pocket need a shoot or 3D. They can be done as stylised 2.5D
    illustration on the Night ground.
  - "Cheque" is British and Commonwealth spelling (US: "check", which collides with "clean-history check"). Keep it to
    visuals and VO. If the team wants US spelling, use "a pre-signed IOU" instead.
  - The metaphor may imply that the *seller* is the villain in every sale. Soften with "could", and make the hole
    structural, not moral.
  - **Accuracy:** "void" must map to Settled, Spent or Expired. "Unfunded" also doesn't block, so don't say "every
    line was cashed". "One transaction pays the seller" is true (failed payouts go to escrow). Don't show a countdown
    on the clasp.

## 4. Treatment B: "Three wallets. One NFT."

**Logline:** A Bitcoin wallet, an Ethereum wallet, a Solana wallet. Are you getting it? It's one NFT, and you can sell
it without anyone keeping a key.

**Approach:** "what if", repetition-reveal (Jobs 2007), pure hypermotion. **Tone:** bright, fast, confident, Apple-keynote
energy on leather. **Target length:** 90 s.

**Central metaphor:** **one object, three keys**. Three chain keys fly into a single strap and become one card. For the
problem: **a house sold while the old owner keeps a spare key** (a key copied before the sale), then a key cabinet
where every key ever cut is logged and the buyer sees each one melted down.

| 0:02–0:04 | | An Ethereum address (`0xD0Cd…d5ad`) whips in from the right. | "An Ethereum wallet." | "An Ethereum wallet." |
| 0:04–0:06 | | A Solana address (`AGSU…XTXd`) drops from the top. | "A Solana wallet." | "A Solana wallet." |

- **Signature moment:** the three addresses snapping onto one card at 0:06–0:11, the repetition-reveal. It carries the
  whole idea by 0:11 with sound off.
- **Music arc:** three rising stabs on the three wallets (tension) → a drop on "It's one NFT." → driving four-on-the-floor
  through the solution montage → a half-time break on "Blocked" → resolve on the handoff.
- **Brand:** the card is the hero of every frame. The brass band is the brand, and the stitch hops only on ownership
  changes. Chain colours appear only as dots (brand rule).
- **VO:** 114 words, about 43–46 s at 150–160 wpm. **Total runtime:** ≈ 90 s (the type and music carry more of it).
- **Risks:**
  - **Too product-forward, too fast**: the hole gets 8 s, and judges could remember "multichain NFT wallet" (which

  - The Jobs "are you getting it?" homage can read as cocky to this jury. A softer variant: "Three wallets. One NFT."
  - **Accuracy:** "Every chain" overreaches in VO (it is three chains). The end-card tagline is the brand's, but don't
    say it in VO. Show the SOL address, not a SOL send. Keep the timer visible around the signer jump-cut.

## 5. Treatment C: "Stitched"

**Logline:** The life of one leather Satchel, from the stitch that says "yours" to the moment it unpicks itself from
the seller and sews onto the buyer, with every signature it ever made written in its ledger.

**Approach:** one-object story, the "craft film". **Tone:** warm, tactile, unhurried at first, then precise; ASMR leather
and brass foley. **Target length:** 105 s.

**Central metaphor:** **the stitch is ownership; the ledger is memory.** The Satchel carries a small ledger. Every
signature is inked in before it is made, the ledger is sealed by a brass clasp while it's for sale, and the buyer reads
every page. Ownership is shown as the stitch physically unpicking from one card and sewing onto the other.

| Time | Beat | Picture | VO | Kinetic text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Hook | Macro: a brass needle pulls a dashed stitch around the edge of a leather card. The band reads "Satchel #1". | "This stitch means you own it." | "This NFT is a wallet." |
| 0:05–0:14 | Idea | Pull back: the card's three chips light up (BTC · ETH · SOL) with real addresses. | "Satchel is an NFT that holds its own Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana addresses. Whoever owns it controls the coins." | "Own the NFT." → "Own the coins." |
| 0:14–0:24 | Problem | A paper slip with 12 seed words passes between two hands; a third hand photographs it. | "Wallets were never made to change hands. Pass on a seed phrase, and the last owner can keep a copy." | "Seed phrases get copied." |
| 0:24–0:32 | First fix | The seed slip turns to dust; the stitch holds. | "A Satchel has no seed phrase. It signs only for its owner." | "Only the owner signs." |
| 0:32–0:42 | Hole | A signature (a wax-sealed note) leaves the card with the old owner, *before* the stitch is unpicked. | "But a signature made before the sale can travel with the old owner, and be spent after it." | "Signed before the sale." |
| 0:42–0:47 | Turn | The buyer opens the Satchel's ledger. | "So every Satchel keeps a ledger." | "Every signature, written down." |
| 0:47–1:02 | Solution | Ink fills a ledger line, *then* the wax seal is pressed (record before signature). Brass clasp closes over the ledger: "Signing paused while listed". | "Each signature is written in before NEAR's signer network makes it. When the Satchel is listed, the clasp shuts: no new signatures." | "Written in first." → "Signing paused while listed" |
| 1:02–1:15 | Check | The buyer's screen: rows resolve one by one, "Settled", "Expired"; the verdict appears; the trust line underneath. | "The buyer's own browser reads every line against each chain, and finds nothing left that can move the coins." | "Clean. No past signature can move these coins." |
| 1:15–1:20 | Negative | Another Satchel: one line still wet ink. "Blocked: 1 unused signature". | "One open line, and there's no Buy." | "Blocked: 1 unused signature" |
| 1:20–1:35 | Signature moment | Buy. The stitch on alice's card unpicks thread by thread; the card lifts, tilts 6°, slides; the thread sews itself around carol's card. "Satchel #3 is yours." | "Clean, so carol buys. The stitch moves, and the wallet goes with it." | "Satchel #3 is yours." |
| 1:35–1:45 | Close | Slow push on the stitched card; end card. | "Satchel. Hand over the wallet, not the risk." | End card |

- **Signature moment:** the stitch unpicking from the seller and sewing onto the buyer. It's the brand's own handoff,
  made physical, and the most ownable image any Satchel film can have.
- **Music arc:** solo cello or a felt piano with leather foley (tension is quiet, almost intimate) → a low swell under
  the hole → a pulse enters with the ledger → strings drive through the check → full resolve at the stitch.
- **Brand:** this is the brand film: leather, brass, the stitch used *only* for ownership, and one brass moment per
  frame (needle, clasp, Buy).
- **VO:** 150 words, about 56–60 s at 150–160 wpm (read slowly, ≈ 65 s). **Total runtime:** ≈ 105 s.
- **Risks:**
  - **Slow first 5 s for X**: the macro needle is beautiful but abstract. The text card must carry "This NFT is a
    wallet." from frame 0.
  - 3D leather and needle work is the most expensive to make in two weeks.
  - "Not the risk" overclaims: clean history doesn't cover post-purchase signatures, the MPC or testnet4 reorgs.
    Prefer "Hand over the wallet. Check every signature."
  - **Accuracy:** wax seals and ink are metaphors. The real product has no stamp or hallmark UI (removed in R3), so
    keep the UI shots honest and the craft shots clearly metaphorical.

## 7. Recommendation: merge B's hook, A's villain and C's stitch

**Working title: "Signed before the sale."** Open with B's repetition-reveal (it carries the idea muted in 10 s), make
the problem A's pre-signed cheque (the most concrete picture of *the* hole and the reason clean history exists), and
resolve with C's stitch and the product's real handoff. Production stays mostly 2D/2.5D motion plus captured fixture
UI; the cheque is one illustrated prop, not a live shoot.

**Hook (0:00–0:05):** three native addresses whip in on the Night ground (BTC, ETH, SOL, each with its chain dot) and
snap onto one brass-banded card. Text: "A Bitcoin wallet." / "An Ethereum wallet." / "A Solana wallet." → **"This NFT is
a wallet."** The brand is on screen by 0:04, and the idea is complete without sound.
