# Seller threat model: every way a seller could try to keep the buyer's value

> Part of the Satchel security docs. The short version, for the coins at the three addresses, is the table in
> [`docs/selling.md`](../selling.md). The trust assumptions are in [`docs/security-model.md`](../security-model.md).

**Scope.** This is an adversarial review of everything a dishonest seller can try in order to end up with value
that should belong to the buyer. The seller may act alone or with help: a delegate they added, a second account of
theirs, a claim link they made, an offer they placed, or the timing of the MPC network. It covers eight areas:
(a) the coins at the Satchel's Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana addresses; (b) the NEAR, USDC and wNEAR held inside the
Satchel on NEAR; (c) the buyer's payment; (d) offers; (e) the app's give-by-link flow; (f) ways to slip a signature
past the buyer's clean-history check; (g) misleading the buyer about what they are buying; and (h) anything else.
Every "what stops it" entry cites the code that does it, and every "tested by" entry names a test function. The
statuses were checked against the code, not the docs. **Covered (tested)**: the code stops it and a test proves
that. **Fixed in this review**: the review found the defence missing, and it was added with tests. **Gap / residual
risk**: nothing in Satchel stops it, by design; each one is a stated trust assumption or limit.

**Path prefixes.** `contract/` = `contracts/satchel/src/`; `core/` = `packages/core/src/`; `web/` = `apps/web/src/`.
Sandbox tests are in `tests/tests/`, contract unit tests in `contracts/satchel/src/*_tests.rs` and `tests.rs`, core
tests in `packages/core/test/`, and web tests in `apps/web/test/unit/` and `apps/web/test/e2e/`.

## Found and fixed in this review

Both were in the app, not the contract.

- **G-1: a send into another Satchel could land on a coin the seller had pre-signed.** The Bitcoin builder avoided
  the txids referenced by the sending Satchel's records, but not the receiving Satchel's. A seller could sign,
  through Satchel B before selling it, a spend of the coin a predictable future send from the buyer's other Satchel
  would create, then broadcast it once the buyer moved coins into B. Every Bitcoin send now looks up the recipient,
  avoids both Satchels' referenced txids, and is not built if the recipient's records can't be read.
- **G-2: "You get" was not the verified balance.** It came from a snapshot cached for up to a minute, so after a
  drain signed before listing reached depth, the page could show a clean history next to coins already gone. It
  now comes from a fresh read in the browser at the check's depth, leaves out encumbered coins, gates Buy, and is
  read once more right before Buy.

## Residual risks

- The MPC network is trusted. A threshold of its nodes could sign anything, and on testnet its contract account has
  full-access keys.
- Data providers are trusted. Two answers are needed when both providers are up, but on testnet one answer is
  accepted when the other is down. One Solana answer is enough, and final verdicts stay cached in the browser.
- Testnet confirmation depth: Bitcoin K = 1 and Ethereum `latest − 3`. A reorg deeper than that can revive a
  signature after its record was judged dead and cached.
- On the development contract, the account has a key and the lock checks are only warnings. Only the final keyless
  contract removes this.
- Coins on other networks at the same script or address, and tokens other than ETH, USDC, SOL, NEAR and wNEAR, are
  not part of a sale and are not checked.
- A claim link is a bearer key. Buying one off-platform has none of the protections of `buy` or `accept_offer`.
- Message signatures can't move coins, but they may still prove control of the address to an off-chain service.
