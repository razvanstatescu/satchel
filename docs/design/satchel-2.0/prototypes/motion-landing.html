No admin key. Not even ours.

Satchel's contract was born without a key and can't be upgraded. Nobody can add one or change its code, us included.

What you still trust: NEAR's signer network, which makes every signature. Satchel is new and hasn't been audited.

Under the hoodThe six rules, and where to check each claim
  1. Plain transfers only. The contract signs only transfers it can read: native ETH on Sepolia, standard Bitcoin spends and plain SOL transfers. It refuses raw hashes, messages, permits and contract calls.
  2. Recorded before it's signed. Each request is written on NEAR, with the nonce, coins or blockhash it can spend, before NEAR's signer network sees it. Timed-out requests stay on the record.
  3. Listing pauses signing. A Satchel can be listed only after 250 blocks (about 2 min 20 s) without signing. While it's for sale, it can't sign, and only a purchase can move it.
  4. The buyer's browser checks every record. It asks each chain whether the nonce was used, the coin spent or the blockhash expired. For Bitcoin and Ethereum two data providers must agree; the page says when only one answered.
  5. Buy stays disabled until it passes. It stays off if any signature could still move coins or can't be checked right now. The app has no override, and checks again just before buying.
  6. One transaction, bound to that history. The purchase names the listing and the signature count the browser checked. If either changed, it fails and refunds; if not, the NFT moves and the seller is paid.

Questions

Why not just send the coins?

You can, one chain at a time. A Satchel hands over Bitcoin and Ethereum together, keeps the same addresses, and lets a buyer pay and take over in one transaction, so neither side sends first.

Who holds the keys?

No single party. NEAR's signer network makes each signature together, only when the Satchel contract asks, and the contract asks only for the NFT's current owner. You trust that network: enough colluding nodes could sign anything, and on testnet that network's own contract account has full-access keys.

What does the buyer's check cover?

Every signature the Satchel has ever made. Your browser, not the contract, checks each one on its own chain, and Buy stays off until none can still move the coins. It doesn't cover other tokens, signatures made after you buy, NEAR's signer network or testnet4 reorgs.

Can the seller sign after I check?

No. A Satchel that's for sale can't sign anything new, and your purchase names the listing and signature count your browser checked. If either changed, it fails and you're refunded.

Which chains and coins work?

Native coins only, no tokens: Bitcoin testnet4 and Ethereum Sepolia. Each Satchel also has a Solana devnet address; the contract can sign SOL transfers, but the app can't send SOL yet. Send only Sepolia ETH to the Ethereum address: coins sent on another network can't be moved.

What does it cost?

Minting takes a 0.02 NEAR deposit and listing 0.01 NEAR, with the unused part refunded. Each signature costs about 0.004 NEAR. Bitcoin and Ethereum fees come out of the Satchel's own coins. Giving one away costs only NEAR gas, and Satchel charges no fee on sales.

Why testnet only? Is it audited?

Satchel is new, unaudited hackathon software. Its contract can't be upgraded, so a bug can't be patched in place, and if NEAR's signer network changed its interface, its Satchels couldn't sign. To try it, use a NEAR testnet wallet such as Meteor or Intear.
